Your town, your money

Hillsborough and Orange County publish their budgets in documents that run to hundreds of pages. This page turns them into the answer you actually want: what they cost your household, what that money pays for, and whether the finances are in good shape.

Nothing here is our opinion. Every figure names the document it came from — and the page, wherever the document has pages — so you can check it yourself. Where a figure comes from something other than a government's own publication, the page says so on the spot.

Who made this, and why you can check it

This page was built for the Orange County Efficiency & Accountability Initiative by residents, not by the town. That is exactly why it shows its sources for everything: you should not have to take our word for a single number. The whole dataset is rebuilt from the town's documents by an open pipeline, and rebuilding it reproduces this page byte for byte.

Where these numbers come from, and how much to trust each one. Most of the audited figures here are read straight out of a proper digital file, with no character recognition involved at all — that is true of FY2018 and of FY2021 through FY2025. Two years, FY2019 and FY2020, exist only as photographs of paper, because that is the form in which the reports were issued.

For those two, the pages are read afresh from the image, and nothing is ever taken from the text layer hidden inside a scan, which scrambles digits — a page that plainly reads 4,610,003 comes back from it as 460,100,3. A recovered figure is published only where its column still adds up exactly to the total printed beside it on the same page. Where a column does not, the cell is left blank rather than filled with a best guess.

That test is strong evidence, and on its own it is not proof — two errors that cancel out would survive it — so it is measured rather than argued. Five years exist in both forms, and every figure is compared between the two: 5,394 figures checked, 5,394 identical, 100.00%. The build refuses to publish at all if that ever stops being true. We would still rather show you less than show you something wrong.
